## I Think I Miss Pair Programming

### Point and structure

- Find the essay's center of gravity: the specific idea everything else should orbit. Here, it is not simply missing human interaction; it is missing the connection created when two people need each other's thinking to solve the same problem.
- Introduce the governing tension early. Agentic engineering has made the work faster and more productive, but it has also made sustained collaboration less necessary.
- Begin with the event that creates the essay's question, not the circumstances in which I encountered it. Lead with Dillon's production incident rather than "I was watching..."
- After using someone else's story, explicitly turn toward my own question. Explain what part of the story I recognize or envy.
- Make structure carry meaning: introduce the tension, show how pairing created connection, show how agents changed that experience, and return to the opening incident in the conclusion.

### Examples and evidence

- Anecdotes should reveal why an event mattered, not merely prove that it happened. The GraphQL story is useful because the lasting memory is the shared discovery, not only the technical solution.
- When examples broaden the essay's central concept, explain what they share. Pull-request reviews and debugging were not formal pairing, but both regularly became sustained, shared problem-solving.
- Give the most space to the part of an example that proves the point. Technical details establish credibility, but the interaction between the people is the subject of this essay.
- A useful pattern for examples: how the work happened before, how agents handle it now, the efficiency gained, and the human interaction lost.

### Clarity and emphasis

- Open sections with their claim rather than a disclaimer about what the section will not cover.
- Distinguish general human interaction from shared attention, mutual dependence, and collaborative problem-solving. Two people talking while their agents work is not the same as two people thinking through a problem together.
- Consolidate repeated qualifications. Instead of repeatedly saying I do not oppose agents, acknowledge once that I do not want to restore the old friction, even though that friction had an accidental human benefit.
- Prefer precise terms over broad abstractions. The loss is not "human interaction" in general; it is the recurring need to invite another person into difficult work.
- Cut conversational throat-clearing such as "With all that said" or "This article isn't meant to..." when the next sentence can state the point directly.

### Grammar and mechanics

- A comma splice occurs when a comma is the only thing joining two independent clauses—two groups of words that could each stand alone as a complete sentence.
- A quick test: read the words on each side of the comma separately. If both are complete sentences, the comma probably needs help.
- Example: `Let me be clear on both of these points, I'm not advocating for slower reviews.` Both halves can stand alone, so the comma creates a splice.
- Fix a comma splice according to the relationship between the ideas:
  - Use a period when each thought deserves its own sentence: `The meeting lasted an hour. I took notes throughout it.`
  - Use a semicolon when the thoughts are closely related: `The syntax confused me; my coworker helped me understand it.`
  - Add a coordinating conjunction such as `and`, `but`, or `so`: `Agents make debugging faster, but they also make it more solitary.`
  - Use a colon when the second clause explains or completes the first: `Let me be clear on one point: I don't want to make debugging slower.`
  - Make one clause dependent: `When I was transitioning to Ruby on Rails, I reviewed a coworker's pull request.`
- A comma before a coordinating conjunction is not a splice: `I understood the syntax, but I remembered the mentorship.`
- Do not choose a fix mechanically. The punctuation should express the relationship between the ideas: separation, connection, contrast, explanation, or dependence.

### Endings

- A conclusion should use the most precise version of the idea the essay discovered.
- Circle back to the opening to create closure. I do not want Dillon's 12-hour incident, but I understand why he remembered it as a great day.
- The essay does not need to invent a solution. It can end by clearly naming the unresolved tradeoff: how do we preserve the efficiency without losing the connection that friction once created?
- Let the ending advance from uncertainty to recognition: "I think I miss pair programming" can become "I miss pair programming."
